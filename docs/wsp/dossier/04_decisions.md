What was decided, against which alternatives, why, what became of it, and what would reverse it.
Reasons are given as they can be defended, so that a reviewer can attack the reason and not only
the outcome. "Outcome" says what the data said afterwards; a decision that was refuted stays in the
log. Ids are stable; new decisions get new ids.

## Method

| Id | Decision | Alternatives weighed | Why | Outcome, and what would reverse it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | A weight patch is a list of literal assignments. No expressions, strides, repetition, in-place arithmetic or includes | The MIT edit language, which has strides and `*=`, `+=` | Each element is written once and is an independent statement a reader can check against its reason | In force. Reverse if a model needs so many equal elements that reading them hides more than it shows |
| D-02 | A release is its weights and its forward. A forward is frozen with its releases; an improvement is a new forward file with new model names | Editing a forward in place and re-identifying its releases | Old release ids, scores and the zero-shot status of old candidates must never change. The four shared forward sources are in every forward hash and are never edited | In force; ten forward files are committed and two more are in progress. Cost: code is duplicated between generations |
| D-03 | `W0` is reserved for a candidate written before any outcome of its fuel or family was seen; later candidates are `D1`, `D2` … and say in their first lines what was read. The lineage root of a data-informed spec is a reference baseline with no data exposure | One naming for all | The owner asked that it be unmistakable which numbers are zero-shot | In force |
| D-04 | Analysis is delegated to other model instances under the author's rules (train only, no fitting); no delegate and no script writes a weight; the author rechecks what he acts on and types every number | Doing all analysis in one context; letting an agent propose numbers | Parallel reading of large train sets; the hand-written claim holds only if one author types the numbers | In force. The recheck was uneven ([03](03_datasets_and_measurements.md)). Reports are saved verbatim from the agents' transcripts |
| D-05 | A candidate is committed before its validation score exists; the adoption rule is written first; validation is read once as an aggregate table and only adopts or vetoes | Tuning on validation; reading validation per run | The validation partitions are small (two sessions per ACME configuration) and would be memorised | In force from phase 2. Broken in phase 1 for FLEX W2 (X-05) and softened once per model in phase 2 (X-06). A rule written before a look stands even for a near miss (BASS D2 was refused over 0.005) |
| D-06 | Spread (band width) is not chosen by the train rule: it is the train residual plus a stated margin for sessions the laws have not seen | Calibrating bands to train coverage | A narrower band always scores better on the runs a law was typed from | Confirmed the hard way: the fourth generation narrowed bands from train coverage and the 90 % band then covered 0.82 and 0.87 on validation. The size of the margin remains a judgment |
| D-07 | Nothing is asked of the other lanes. What a model needs is built here, in a private copy of the data root, or the model does without and says so. Shared contracts and records are untouched | Requirement lists for the data lane | The owner's instruction of 14 November | In force. It created the private-root risk described in 2.6 of the data page |
| D-08 | Every candidate, release, card and feedback file is kept; a refused candidate stays as a negative result | Cleaning up | The negative results are the evidence for what does not transfer | In force |

## Droplets (FLEX)

| Id | Decision | Alternatives weighed | Why | Outcome, and what would reverse it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-10 | One model per fuel. For n-heptane: freeze cohort and split from metadata, freeze a literature-only W0, only then open train outcomes | One droplet model with a fuel input | The fuels differ in kind (water uptake against cool flames); a clean zero-shot reference was still possible for heptane | W0 scores 60.3 µm on validation against 105 for the D²-law; it stays in the tables |
| D-11 | A droplet is in one of three states (hot flame, cool flame, no flame); extinction is a mixture of a diffusive branch decided by burn progress and a radiative branch decided by the clock `s = t · 1 mm²/s / D0²`, split at a critical diameter | One distribution of extinction diameters, as in phase 1 | The train series show two separate branches of `Dext/D0` and a size threshold at fixed atmosphere (M-01) | Carried through every generation; it is what transferred. Would be reversed by train pairs at one atmosphere that extinguish on the "wrong" side by a wide margin |
| D-12 | A validity gate from physics in place of a start time: a prefix value is dropped when at least three later values of D² exceed it by more than 5 %, or lie below what a 2 mm²/s flame could have burned | Discarding the first half second | The early "growth" is igniter glare in the 2009 runs and soot shells in heptane; the gate removes exactly those frames and uses only data up to the cutoff | In force. The constants 5 % and 2 mm²/s are judgments (twice the fastest measured rate) |
| D-13 | A run whose conditions are stored as zero or marked as conflicting is refused, not guessed | Imputing | A forecast on a made-up atmosphere is worse than none | In force: FLEX-114, H19H101, H19H201 |
| D-14 | Third generation: radiative loss as a cliff in the clock (−3.8 per unit of ln(D0/D_crit)); the run's rate offset trusted far less at short cutoffs; the state at the cutoff read from the end rate of the prefix; a faster evaporation transient after the flame | Keeping the second-generation slopes | Train audits (M-03), each rechecked | Adopted: validation 45.3 → 40.0 µm (methanol), 51.9 → 46.1 µm (heptane). These are the carried models |
| D-15 | Fourth generation: sharper class boundary, narrower bands, tracker flags, a helium after-burn law | — | Train anatomies and the literature review | **Refused by validation**, all three candidates (44.8, 40.1, 57.6 µm against 39.5 and 46.1). The helium law was typed as `exp(3.85 x_He)` with nothing bounding it at the hot rate: an error visible without validation. Lesson recorded in PROTOCOL section 9 |
| D-16 | Droplet position is not added to the prefixes | Ingesting drift as an observation | It would change the input hash of every FLEX prefix of the shared dataset and break pairing with every earlier release | In force; a droplet that will leave the view is not foreseen. The private root makes this reversible now |
| D-17 | Fifth generation: read the flame cameras in the prefix. A flame seen in the last quarter second sets the state to hot; no flame seen sets it to not hot, with trust 0.95; numbers are instrument facts (frame rates, a minimum of five frames), not train statistics | A Bayes rule with a per-frame detection probability (tried first, could not overrule a law prior of 1e-8 coherently); using flame size as a predictor (M-07 says it does not separate) | The cameras observe the state the model otherwise infers from the diameter | Train: methanol −0.97 µm, heptane +0.08 µm (fifth forward). Carried into the P1 candidates of the sixth forward, which were **not adopted** on validation (methanol 42.5 against 39.5 µm; heptane 48.4 against 46.1, and 1.2 µm worse than the same laws without cameras). The cameras have shown no gain on unseen days. Attack: C-12 |
| D-18 | Fifth generation: a heavy-tailed radiative clock, `S_R(s) = (1 − ε) exp(−Λ_R(s)) + ε / (1 + s/s_R)` with ε = 0.1 | Leaving the clock log-normal-like with zero survival past it | When a camera shows a hot flame that the clock says is impossible (FLEX-018, 2 atm CO2: the UV camera shows the flame until 8.0 s; the model's clock ends at 0.37 of that and at 3 and 5 s it gives probability 1.0 to the flame being gone), the class probabilities collapse to nonsense | Costs 0.2 to 0.3 µm on train without cameras. ε is a judgment ("one droplet in ten outlives the law"), from one run. Part of the P1 candidates that were not adopted; the 90 % band of heptane P1 covered 0.88 on validation |
