# 3. What was measured on the data, and how far it was checked

Every result read from the datasets, with who measured it, where the table is, and an honest status.
"Rechecked" means the author recomputed the quantity from the train files or tables himself.
"Spot-checked" means he looked at some of the runs behind it. "On trust" means the report was used
as written. A reviewer should spend time in proportion to how much a model leans on a row and how
little it was checked.

All measurements are on the train partition unless the row says otherwise. Nothing in this page was
fitted: scripts print finite differences, window means, counts, medians and ranges.

## 3.2 Classifications made inside measurements

**Astra review addendum, 14 November.** F-001 independently reproduces M-10's 99 early
endings and 29 scripted endings from train-only measurement tables; this verifies their
arithmetic, not the original channel assignment or pre-ignition file provenance. F-003
independently evaluates the methanol property equations at two temperatures; it does not
reproduce the coupled transient or the train-window measurements of M-20. No status for
those wider claims is promoted. All uncertain raw material from the F-004 alias check is
excluded from model design. No new validation or test measurement was made.

These are choices, not facts. Each could move a count.

**Ending kinds of a droplet's hot flame (M-07).** (c) cool flame: the droplet keeps shrinking at
0.20 mm²/s or more over both 0.2 to 3.2 s and 3.2 to 8.2 s after the flame. Otherwise (a) radiative
when the droplet diameter at flame end over D0 is at least 0.65, (b) diffusive or burn-out below
it. (d) cannot tell: an indirect diameter ratio between 0.55 and 0.75, or a heptane droplet not seen
afterwards. The diameter at flame end is the measured one when the droplet is tracked, else NASA's
`Dext`, else a value from NASA's `kavg`; 21 of the (b) calls rest on the last two. Flame end is the
later of the two cameras. One run, FLEX-182, was called (a) by a margin of 0.002 mm²/s against a
narrative that says cool flame. The agent set the thresholds "from the gaps the docs describe".

**Spurious flame frames (M-07 and the forward).** The agent dropped any frame more than 1.6 times
its neighbours. The fifth-generation forward does not use that rule: it takes the last frame that
has two more positive frames within a quarter second before it.

**Condition cells of the shift map (M-06).** Pressure, oxygen, fuel-fraction and diameter bands are
listed at the end of the report. With other edges the counts "in a populated cell" change. For
inverse flames (0 of 24) the statement does not depend on the edges for 15 runs, whose ambient fuel
fraction is below every train value (and the injected oxygen fractions 0.40, 0.45 and 0.70 never
occur in train). The other nine are propane in a rich ambient at 0.5 and 1.1 atm, where train has
ethane and n-butane only: they are novel as long as the fuel is part of the cell.

**Stop classes of an ACME test (M-08).** Extinction-triggered, scripted end, limit abort, ended
without stamps (taken as limit trips from the temperature), no timeline file. Five extinction-
triggered tests are labelled flow termination in the table.

**Regimes of the train tables (M-01, M-02).** The critical diameter per atmosphere is a bracket
between two train droplets, not a measured point; the narratives' words ("diffusively
extinguishes", "radiatively extinguishing", "outside the quasi-steady flammability boundary") were
used as labels and agree with the brackets.

## 3.4 What was looked for and not found

Reported here because a later reader tends to rediscover them: no effect of the order of burns
within a chamber fill, of drift speed, or of CO2 and pressure on the methanol rate beyond the typed
law; the burner's start temperature does not depend on the order of tests in a day (24.9 to 25.2 °C
by rank); the first five seconds of burner heating do not settle the later rise; the first seconds
of the flame's size do not say whether a droplet will lose its flame early.
