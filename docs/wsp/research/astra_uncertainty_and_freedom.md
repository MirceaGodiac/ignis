# Astra: Uncertainty, ensembles, and the freedom in hand-written models

**14 November 2026.** This is an audit of what literal authorship does and does not buy. It is not a new weight set, an evaluation, or a claim that the carried models are calibrated. All carried releases are declared data-informed in the local dossier. No weights, fitting, searches, validation-series outcomes, test/holdout materials, or published per-test results were read or generated for this note.

## The useful distinction is provenance, not parameter count

A typed number is an authorship fact: someone entered it. It does not tell us whether the number was a handbook constant, a direct apparatus specification, a law parameter inferred by solving train equations, a choice made after comparing variants, or an uncertainty width. “No optimizer ran” is a valuable execution constraint but not an inferential firewall. Solving a formula from two observed runs, choosing a row after seeing outcomes, or choosing among many releases after reading a score is data adaptation even if every arithmetic operation is transparent and the last value was typed by a person.

The N/A/L/S/U table in the dossier is useful for tracing why a number exists. It is not an accounting theorem. There is no universally valid scalar degrees-of-freedom count for a code-forward plus hand-authored constants. A property block of many externally sourced entries may have little direct dependence on this campaign, while one threshold can encode a sharp decision from a handful of training cases. The total adaptive procedure also includes model family, feature and state choices, source selection, table-row selection, ranges, gates, scenarios, variants considered, and decisions prompted by development feedback. If a model is to claim a complexity or generalization bound, one must define an encoding or hypothesis procedure, the sampling assumptions, and what feedback the author saw. A hand count of constants cannot stand in for that.

External constants can still be a strong scientific design choice. A value traced to a canonical source, in the correct units and domain, can replace a campaign-specific coefficient and make extrapolation more physically constrained. It lowers one identifiable route of direct outcome dependence. It does not validate the mechanism, remove structure choices, capture source uncertainty, or guarantee transfer to a new apparatus/session. For a property table, record exact source edition/table/row, species/phase/temperature/pressure range, unit conversion, interpolation, rounding, and uncertainty. Test identities and independent cross-checks can catch transcription errors; they cannot certify the model's predictive calibration.

## What an ensemble identity actually says

For two scalar predictions `a,b` and a squared-error target `y`, the squared loss of the mean is exactly the mean of member losses minus `(a-b)^2/4`. The same is true for Brier loss when `a,b` are binary probabilities. This is an algebraic per-case fact. It gives no guarantee of improvement over the best member, future observations, or a shifted regime. “Members differ in kind” is not a condition of the identity; it is an interpretive argument for retaining scenarios.

For distributions, a committee should specify whether it reports the arithmetic mean of member summaries or the mixture distribution. CRPS is the integrated squared distance between forecast CDF and the step CDF at the observation. Therefore an equal mixture of distributions `F` and `G` obeys the exact identity `mean(CRPS(F,y), CRPS(G,y)) - CRPS((F+G)/2,y) = ¼∫(F(t)-G(t))²dt`. The disagreement is an integrated CDF difference, not a difference between means. As with Brier, the mixture is no worse than the average member score on that same case, but it need not beat the better member or generalize better under shift. Propriety is a statement about expected score when the forecast is the true distribution, not a blanket empirical guarantee.

Equal weights can be a clear, predeclared way to expose unresolved model disagreement. They are not automatically a probability that each physical story is true. If member weights are adjusted based on scores, those scores become part of the adaptive evidence and need to be disclosed. Structured expert-judgment methods provide one instructive contrast: they elicit probabilistic quantiles and use out-of-sample calibration questions with a proper score to assess and combine experts. That is a formal validation procedure, not a license to call untested author scenarios calibrated.

## Uncertainty without fit and without a coverage promise

Honest uncertainty may be represented by distinct sources, but each requires an interpretation:

- **Measurement uncertainty:** instrument precision, calibration and segmentation error, with stated units and any correlations.
- **Input uncertainty:** true session oxygen/flow, initial state, timing, or other latent condition. Use physically justified ranges or explicitly typed nodes; do not disguise an unknown distribution as a measured one.
- **Mechanism uncertainty:** alternative laws or mechanisms, labeled as scenarios and not silently averaged into a posterior.
- **Residual/session variability:** only claim a predictive distribution if the assumptions and evidence for how residuals vary across sessions are stated.
- **Target/observation uncertainty:** label ambiguity, censoring, competing causes and field-of-view loss, kept distinct from physical forecast uncertainty.

A deterministic band built from residuals plus a judgment margin is a useful engineering interval, but absent a sampling model and a validated calibration procedure it is not a 90% predictive interval. A finite set of scenario nodes with typed weights is not automatically a posterior either. Prior-predictive checks offer a useful no-fit discipline: simulate or analytically inspect what the proposed law implies over physical regimes before comparing against outcomes, and reject implausible consequences. They check implications of assumptions, not whether the assumptions have the right probabilities. The cited Bayesian-workflow treatment concerns probability distributions over parameters; in IGNIS WSP, its transferable principle is checking the consequences of assumptions, not adopting fitted Bayesian machinery or claiming Bayesian coverage.

For new predictive uncertainty, report ranges as **engineering bounds**, **elicited beliefs**, or **scenario probabilities**, whichever they actually are. Distinguish those from observed empirical coverage. Calibrated coverage needs assumptions such as exchangeability and a procedure designed for it; session shifts, repeated model revision, clustered chamber operations, and informative stopping can break those assumptions. No coverage guarantee follows from hand-writing, physics plausibility, or a proper scoring rule alone.

## Censoring and competing termination causes

An event forecast should specify the target, cause, horizon, and information time. “Burn ends by time `t`” is not a single event if self-extinction, operator/script stop, and observation cutoff have different meanings. If an actual stop is an admissible prospective input, a forecast conditional on it answers a different question from self-extinction risk. For a self-extinction-specific target, a known script stop is a competing event; for an all-termination target, it is an event of another cause. An observation cutoff before the target horizon is right-censoring. These statuses must not be conflated. Proper Brier-type event scoring needs status at the horizon; censored cases need a method that accounts for censoring assumptions rather than treating them as event-free. Competing risks require cumulative incidence or a clear cause-specific target, not a cause-specific hazard casually interpreted as absolute risk.

The burner-flame model context also includes a strong time horizon (6–120 seconds in the dossier) and script-derived stopping information. That makes “accuracy” conditional on the target definition and input contract. Score the forecast for the question actually being asked, and state whether it predicts all termination, self-extinction, or planned stop.

## How to grow without pretending growth is free

The largest defensible hand-written model is not defined by a maximum tensor size. It is the largest mechanistic construction whose pieces remain traceable and whose uncertainties and failure modes remain inspectable. A resolved transport solver may have many typed property values, but discretization and boundary conditions are structural choices; code may implement the equations but must not secretly estimate their parameters. A shared property library can prevent inconsistent constants and make one error visible across families, provided each property remains source- and domain-qualified. Instrument models can improve forecasts only when their observation/availability mechanisms are represented honestly. A cross-family forward is a scientific hypothesis about shared mechanisms, not merely a refactor.

A practical authoring discipline:

1. Keep each entry's provenance separate: external property, apparatus specification, measured apparatus value, law parameter, structural choice, or uncertainty judgment. Add a separate data-dependence flag: zero-shot, external-data-informed, train-informed, validation-informed, or exposed/uncertain.
2. Record the exact typed number and exact physical reason, with units, valid domain, precision rationale, and independent derivation where feasible. For calculation-by-hand values, show equations and inputs; label any campaign observation in them as data-informed.
3. Write a forward-level physical envelope before looking at outcomes: conservation, positivity, limiting behavior, dimensional consistency, monotonicity only where supported, and behavior at regimes beyond the training grid. These are prior-predictive/analytic checks, not predictive validation.
4. Expose structure separately from constants: number of state classes, gates, terms, scenario branches, thresholds, and variant decisions. Avoid collapsing scientific choices into “code is free.”
5. For a committee, state members, weights, event target, and why each scenario exists. Equal weight is a transparent default under ignorance, not a data-derived truth probability.
6. Preserve all attempted candidates and their exposure records. A discarded candidate and a score-informed choice are part of the history that determines how adaptive the final model was.
7. Reserve clean evaluation for a frozen protocol and a steward who does not author the candidates. Reused aggregate validation is development feedback even when no per-case score is viewed.

## What the assigned claims support

The ensemble algebra in C-27 is valid for mean scalar point predictions under squared error, probability forecasts under Brier loss, and equally mixed CDF forecasts under CRPS, relative to the average member score on the same cases. “Different in kind” is a reasoned design criterion, not a theorem. The non-significant paired comparison in C-45 does not prove equivalence: equivalence needs a prespecified practical margin and sufficient precision for the interval to sit wholly inside it. The current dossier itself says there are nine validation days, repeated looks, and paired ranges broad enough to include meaningful differences; the defensible conclusion is unresolved comparison.

C-33 is a hypothesis suggested by nine adopt/refuse decisions that were reviewed by the same author who formulated the rule. It is valuable as a retrospective design principle, but not a validated predictor of what will generalize. A genuinely outcome-blind recoding could assess whether the rule describes the historical candidates, after freezing the complete candidate set and coding rubric; it cannot turn already reused validation partitions into a fresh test of transfer.

The allowed exposure ledger sharpens the limits. X-01 explicitly says the BASS-II SIBAL cohort tables and boundaries reached an earlier dossier before the D1 quench law was typed; this is enough to bar describing that boundary as an independent blind transfer check. X-02 and X-03 put whole-campaign FLEX/ACME aggregates into author context, so later choices need exposure-aware wording even where a patch cites train. X-04 is unresolved context exposure through mixed CFI-G sources. X-14–X-17 and X-19 are disclosed minor/result-fragment exposures; the ledger says no associated number is known to have been typed, which is weaker than proving no influence. X-18 warns that conditions of consecutive tests can encode a preceding outcome, so “inputs only” must be checked for dependency, not assumed safe by field label. The reviewer should not inspect the contaminated original cohort dossiers or protected data to repeat these facts; the allowed ledger is the source used here.
